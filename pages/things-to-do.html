A little different, in the best way

A day here can go in any direction.

Stirling-Rawdon offers an easy mix of arts, wellness experiences, agricultural history, countryside scenery and people who take pride in their businesses. From a yoga workshop or theatre seat to a quiet picnic stop, you can shape a visit around the pace that suits you.

Start with these local favourites, then leave time for the unexpected discoveries.

A reason to visit Stirling-Rawdon

Come for the theatre.
Stay for the village.

Some evenings are worth planning a trip around.

At Stirling Festival Theatre, professional live performances take centre stage in a historic village building dating to 1927. From plays and music to comedy and the much-loved annual pantomime, the theatre draws visitors from communities across Ontario.

The experience is close-knit and full of character, with a 419-seat main-stage auditorium and a smaller space for intimate performances and dinner theatre. It is one of Stirling-Rawdon's distinctive cultural destinations.

Heritage & history

Step into Stirling's story.

At Farmtown Park, the Hastings County Museum of Agricultural Heritage, a recreated village brings rural Ontario in the 1930s and 1940s to life. Wander past a general store, print shop, one-room schoolhouse and old-fashioned businesses, then discover vintage tractors, farm equipment and a collection of miniature vehicles.

It's a place to see how communities like Stirling grew, not just read about them.

Farmtown Park · 437 West Front Street · Seasonal admission

The old railway station

The restored station at 122 North Street is a reminder of the railway years that shaped the village. Today the building is associated with a seasonal antique shop and visitor information. Check opening details before going inside.

Slow down, step outside

Fresh air is never far away.

One of the pleasures of Stirling-Rawdon is how quickly the village gives way to a creek, a leafy path or a quiet stretch of countryside.

Henry Street Park & the covered bridge

A family-friendly park at 85 Henry Street sits beside Rawdon Creek. The nearby covered bridge and village streets make an easy addition to a relaxed walk through town.

Station Park & the Heritage Trail

Follow the old railway corridor from the village. Station Park sits near the former train station, with a trail connection for a peaceful walk or cycle.

King's Mill Conservation Area

On Wellmans Road, this quiet conservation property has views of Squires Creek and a historic grist mill. The mill is closed to visitors; the grounds are better suited to photography, nature appreciation and a picnic than a marked hike.

Trails & outdoors

Take the long way around.

Start in the village, follow an old rail corridor or wander a little further into the countryside. These walks make it easy to see another side of Stirling-Rawdon.

An easy village walk

Rawdon Creek & the covered bridge

Begin at Henry Street Park, 85 Henry Street. Walk past Rawdon Creek toward the covered bridge and downtown shops. The Township's family-friendly walking route connects the creek, playground and village streets, with plenty of places to stop for a break.

Read the Township's village route ↗ Start at Henry Street Park ↗
Former railway corridor

Heritage Trail from North Street

Start near the former railway station at 122 North Street. The Township describes trail access near North Street and a walk east along the old railway bed, past village streets toward a wooded stretch and bridge. Its published route is roughly 2 km; check the route and conditions before setting out.

Read the Township's railway walk ↗ Find Station Park on the map ↗

Prefer a quiet country stop? King's Mill Conservation Area has views of Squires Creek and the historic mill from the grounds. It is not a marked hiking route, and the mill itself is closed to the public.

King's Mill visitor guidance ↗

Trail conditions, surfaces and access can change with weather and season. Follow posted signs, check the Township's route descriptions, and take care at road crossings.

Yoga, workshops & wellness

Creekside Yoga

Make a class part of your visit to Stirling. Set beside Rawdon Creek in a bright Mill Street studio, Creekside Yoga offers drop-in yoga, beginner programs, specialty workshops and private sessions. Their classes and pre-registered courses are another reason to spend time in the village.
