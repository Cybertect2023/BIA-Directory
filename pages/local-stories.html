The people behind the curtain

Fun.

Stirling Festival Theatre
Interview submitted June 2025

Meet Ken MacDougall

A stage, a village, and the joy of making people laugh.

For Ken MacDougall, Stirling wasn't a place he visited once. It was somewhere he kept coming back to, until it became home.

“I first came to Stirling in 1999 as an actor. I kept coming back and moved here in 2017.”

Ken MacDougall · 2025 BIA interview

Ken described Stirling Festival Theatre as an arts and entertainment organization serving Hastings County, one that brings people into the village from the GTA, Ottawa, Kingston, Peterborough and surrounding communities. That connection between the stage and the local economy makes the theatre much more than somewhere to buy a ticket.

He spoke warmly about the interaction between performers and audience members, especially the theatre's pantomimes. When asked to capture the spirit of the organization in one word, his answer was simple: “Fun”.

Ken also reflected on the challenges live entertainment faced during the pandemic and his pride in how the theatre recovered. Looking toward 2027, he shared hopes for celebrations around the historic theatre building's centennial. The theatre's official website carries the latest plans and performance information.

The hands behind the product

Made.

Rawdon Creek Soap Co.
Interview submitted August 2025

Meet Julie Amodeo

Handcrafted in Stirling. Rooted in its community.

A bar of soap can tell a bigger story: local ingredients, independent makers and a decision to create something meaningful close to home.

“Everything we do is real—from our locally sourced materials to the way we interact with our customers and community.”

Julie Amodeo · 2025 BIA interview

Rawdon Creek Soap Co. makes handcrafted soaps, candles and skincare products. Julie's road to the business was anything but a straight line. In her interview, she described 30 years as a hairstylist and years working in real estate, experiences that taught her how much lasting customer relationships matter.

What drew her to the business was the chance to make practical, thoughtfully crafted products and connect with the people who use them. That local connection extends into the ingredients and the store itself. Julie said the company sources water buffalo milk locally and makes an effort to support and stock other regional artisans.

For Julie, the word that captures the business is “Authentic.” It's reflected in the small-batch approach and in the belief that buying from one local maker helps sustain others.

A different kind of local service

Grit.

Blue Collar Therapy
Interview submitted September 2025 as Parsons Therapy

Meet Brad Parsons

Building a place where real conversations feel possible.

Brad Parsons wanted to create counselling that felt accessible to the farmers, tradespeople, families and shift workers who call rural Ontario home.

“Be real. Be honest. Don’t overcomplicate what doesn’t need complicating.”

Brad Parsons · 2025 BIA interview

Brad's career took him through labour-intensive jobs and community-facing work before counselling. In his interview, he described seeing how easily stress and difficult experiences could go unspoken, especially among people accustomed to handling everything themselves.

He wanted a different approach: straightforward conversations, respect for rural life and support that doesn't feel out of place in a small town. He described Stirling as a community with an unusually strong sense of work ethic and neighbourliness, and as the place he chose to put down roots.

When asked to describe the spirit of his business in a single word, Brad chose “grit.” The business was called Parsons Therapy when this interview was submitted in 2025. Its current brand is Blue Collar Therapy, where Brad continues to be listed on the team.
